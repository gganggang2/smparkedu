This study examined patterns of test and academic stress among Korean high school students and identified correlates of profile membership at both student and school levels using multilevel latent profile analysis. Data were drawn from the seventh wave of the Korean Educational Longitudinal Study 2013 (KELS 2013), which surveyed second-year high school students. Three student-level stress profiles were identified: high (38.07%), moderate (48.55%), and low (13.38%). Two school-level profiles emerged: high-stress–dominant schools (63.57%) and moderate-stress–dominant schools (36.43%). More study time, a stronger preference for competitive learning, and being female were associated with a greater likelihood of belonging to the high-stress student profile, whereas higher self-management and more positive teacher and peer relationships were associated with a greater likelihood of belonging to the low-stress profile. At the school level, location in mid-sized cities, autonomous high schools and vocational high school types, and higher levels of individualized instruction were associated with moderate-stress–dominant schools, while higher levels of teacher achievement pressure, teacher enthusiasm, and school-average household income were associated with high-stress–dominant schools. These findings highlight the need for school-level efforts to mitigate student stress, such as reducing achievement pressure and expanding individualized instruction, alongside students’ own coping strategies.
