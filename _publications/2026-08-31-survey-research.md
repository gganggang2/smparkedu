This study examined the longitudinal reciprocal relations between self-esteem and academic helplessness among high school students at both the within-person and between-person levels. In addition, the study investigated whether these relations differed by gender and whether perceived parenting styles played a significant role in these relations. A Random Intercept Cross-Lagged Panel Model (RI-CLPM) was applied to data from KCYPS 2018, spanning the 1st through 3rd years of high school. The results were as follows. First, at the within-person level, all autoregressive effects of self-esteem and academic helplessness were statistically significant. Among the cross-lagged effects, only academic helplessness in the 2nd year of high school negatively predicted self-esteem in the 3rd year. Second, gender differences in the within-person longitudinal reciprocal relations between self-esteem and academic helplessness were not significant. Third, at the between-person level, both positive and negative parenting styles had significant effects on the time-invariant trait components of self-esteem and academic helplessness.
